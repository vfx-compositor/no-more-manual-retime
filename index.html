Rebuild the retime from the pictures.

Bring the offline edit and camera plate into Nuke as Reads, select them, and run. With the defaults untouched, image-only analysis finds the retime and builds the TimeWarp, reframe (scale, position, flop) and QC tree. No EDL, XML or matching timecode required.

04Close03Run
  1. Bring the offline edit and camera plate into the script as Read nodes, then select both.
  2. Run NMMR → No More Manual Retime. The selected Reads fill EDIT and PLATE; leave the defaults as they are.
  3. Press Run. Image-only analysis finds the retime without TC calibration. Burn-in TC and split regions are optional tools for cases that need them; Look Match (Beta) is on by default.
  4. Read the score and warnings in the report, then scrub the QC difference.

The tool supports Korean and English. New Reads inherit your Nuke label, font and color settings. The UI demonstrations use illustrative scores and follow the page language; measured accuracy is reported in the benchmark section.

03QC review

QC review

Turn on QC review and the finished results are joined into one movie or JPEG sequence. It is for a quick visual check, so it suits previews as well as a final pass; the size can be FHD (default), HD, original or half of original.

  • Look match + Difference (Beta), the default: the edit and the retimed plate are tone-matched first, so it is dark where they agree and bright only where they still differ.
  • Plain Difference: compares without the tone match.
  • Structure overlay: lays amplified structure over a dimmed edit.
  • Reading it: bright outlines are where to look. A dark frame alone does not prove the timing.

Look Match is only there to make QC easier. An edit is usually graded and the plate is not, so a plain Difference lights up across the whole frame and buries the real timing errors. Look Match fits one fixed Grade to the plate so the Difference shows mainly what really differs. It is not meant to deliver the edit’s look, and it never changes the TimeWarp keys or the found frames.

  • On or off: QC look match (Beta) in Advanced options (on by default).
  • After a run: disable the NMMR_look_* Grade to compare or render without it.

The match score

Each plate gets an image-similarity score derived from its matching cost. A displayed 100% does not prove pixel identity or correct timing. This score is separate from benchmark frame accuracy.

LowFairGood
0%70%85%100%
Low · marked “Review required”

Check the offline for rotation, heavy reframing or VFX already in it, then exclude those frames or pin them.

Fair · about 70–85%

In the QC difference, step through the frames the report lists.

Good · about 85% and up

This measures image similarity, not frame accuracy. Check the QC Difference across the sequence even when the score is good.

The score is separate from the warnings. A shot can match well overall and still contain one suspected cut; the cut warning says where. A [!] low confidence warning appears when image-only analysis cannot pin the frames down, even if the score looks fine. Warning coverage is reported separately and is not counted as frame accuracy.

The numbers, in detail

The earlier development and regression set contains 394 edit conditions, replayed in two modes (image-only and image + burn-in TC) for 788 runs in total (cached CPU replay outside Nuke, first recorded with an earlier development engine). The development-set general-retime figure (separate from the shots-not-used headline above) combines two groups fixed in advance: 82 existing conditions from 17 source clips (explicit holds and reverse playback excluded) and 96 added conditions from 12 source clips (holds and reverse playback included). Together that is 29 source clips, 178 edit conditions and 11,506 frames per mode, with 99.29% exact frames for image-only analysis and 99.84% for image + burn-in TC analysis. All 178 conditions were measured on the same engine. Low-motion and special conditions are reported separately. Accuracy for additional footage and special conditions is reported separately. These figures come from data used during development and do not guarantee accuracy on new footage; results in an actual Nuke session may differ.

Measured through 2026-10-25 with the frozen engine. The cached CPU ledger contains 394 recorded edit conditions replayed in image-only and image + burn-in TC modes: 788 runs and 68,940 scored frames across both modes. It is not a fresh-render or actual-GUI count. The existing 82 general-retime conditions and the added 96 were later replayed together on a newer engine (538 conditions, 1,076 runs, no errors); the existing 82 gave the same exact-frame counts as in the ledger above.

  • A very small fixed zoom (1.02×) is still missed, while the measured 1.03× and 1.04× cases are 100.00%. This is a spatial-transform limitation; the product’s primary completion target is correct frame correspondence.
  • In the Single tab, choose each plate and drag its area in the edit. A separate TC area can be assigned to each plate. The pre-run check distinguishes readable digits, source TC range compatibility and image correspondence; matching numbers alone do not confirm a source. Missing evidence is held for review, and changed inputs invalidate the result. Automatic region search remains available when no area is set. Batch stays in ordinary single-plate mode; reframed split output and QC Look Match on partial regions remain held. Checks cover a synthetic two-source composite with different source frame rates, not arbitrary production composites. Ordinary retime accuracy figures do not measure split-screen performance.
  • Look Match (Beta) is on by default. It locally renders the final retime and spatial transform on 6–12 trusted corresponding frames, estimates one fixed Grade for the shot, and accepts it only when separate validation frames improve. When it validates, an enabled Grade NMMR_look_<plate> is added under the Transform in the output tree and the QC Difference compares after it. The Grade is there to make the QC comparison clearer, not to deliver the edit’s look; disable it to render the original tones. When it is held, no Grade is made. The TimeWarp keys and the reframe Transform stay unchanged. It is deferred for split/region cases or when there is not enough evidence, and the extra local renders add processing time. Nuke checks cover Grade math, graph wiring, disable comparison, cancellation and save/reload. Across 512 replay runs, frame paths, fractional lookups and spatial outputs remained unchanged.
  • A look match is not proof that the frame is correct: a nearby wrong frame can also take a Grade. Secondary QC (Beta) is a separate Advanced option, off by default. It rerenders the final QC inputs locally at analysis resolution for every output frame and flags brightness differences for review. It adds rendering time, preserves the retime keys, and reports limitations for static imagery or incomplete measurements. It does not automatically correct frames or certify frame accuracy. Detailed measurements are saved inside the QC node. Per-plate regions and pre-run TC checks remain separate Beta features.

The target is the encoder setting. The measured range comes from the generated first and second test batches, so short or complex clips can land above it.

Limits

What it won't do

Search range

  • Rotation is not foundRotation is not detected. Exclude or pin rotating sections.
  • Scale outside 0.8–1.6× is not searchedScale outside 0.8–1.6× is not searched, except pillarboxed edits whose picture is under 70% of the frame width: that area is matched on its own.
  • Jump cuts inside a shot are flagged, not followedIf the edit skips frames inside a shot, the path does not jump with it. It stays one continuous speed curve, reverse included, and the cut is flagged. Pin or exclude that stretch on the timeline to fix it.
  • High speeds stop at a ceilingThe default user ceiling is 16 plate frames per edit frame (adjustable from 4 to 16). The search begins at 4 and expands only when sustained high-speed evidence supports it. This ratio is not real-time speed when plate and edit FPS differ, and the benchmark does not claim 98% accuracy across every high-speed condition. Short jumps can still look like cuts: two- or three-frame jumps are not flagged, and real-footage four- and eight-frame jumps went unflagged without readable timecode.

Hard footage

  • Zoom on a near-still plateA zoom or drift on a plate that barely changes (a slow tilt) cannot be told apart from a time shift. The tool warns “time/position ambiguous” (cannot tell a time difference from camera movement) and the path may wobble; pin two or three frames.
  • Near-still stretches: warned, not hiddenNear-still stretches can still be wrong, but they raise a warning. On 23 synthetic edit conditions with known answers (46 runs), all 3 runs that finished under 90% frame accuracy raised the orange “Insufficient evidence” warning. In a wider test set, the same rule also fired on 2 of the 233 runs that finished at 90% or better (one slowed zoom-out condition, in both modes). The path is left as image analysis found it, so the warning asks you to check, not to redo it. Burn-in timecode or two pins at the ends of the stretch settle it.
  • Interpolated slow motion is still weakSlow motion whose in-between frames were invented by interpolation (Kronos and similar) is still weak. Two of the test edits with known answers show it: a 0.75× Kronos edit landed within half a frame on 82.2% of frames with image-only analysis, and a 0.5× interpolated slow motion combined with a zoom on 56.7% (100% with burn-in timecode added).

Output, QC and setup

  • Whole plate frames outRetimes are whole plate frames, except in interpolated slow stretches, where the TimeWarp carries fractional frame values. The default output renders the lower of the two plate frames. Frame blend and OFlow output are selectable, and Kronos needs NukeX. How closely the Frame blend and OFlow pictures match the edit was not measured.
  • Edit-only burn-ins show in QCBurn-ins that exist only in the edit leave faint bright edges in the QC review even on a correct retime. Judge by large, structured bright areas.
  • Look Match is a fixed Grade, validatedLook Match (Beta) creates at most one fixed Grade per shot, enabled on the output tree only when separate validation frames improve. It does not change the TimeWarp keys or the reframe Transform, prove that a frame is correct, or run a second residual-based correction pass. Disable the Grade to render the original look. The primary result remains the recovered plate-frame correspondence.
  • Processing time variesProcessing time depends on plate length, edit length and conditional precision work. Across 33 cached CPU runs of the frozen engine on 11 selected conditions, per-condition medians ranged from 3.84 to 11.81 s on the stated machine. These wall times include cache/pickle loading and module import but exclude Nuke rendering, media preparation and GUI work; they are not a per-shot GUI timing promise.
  • File discovery needs path setupAutomatic file discovery is off until paths are configured. You can instead select EDIT and PLATE Reads directly, without path setup. The files must be accessible and readable by Nuke. Check configured paths with Test with current script. See Path setup.

Timecode

  • Plate TC labels can be one frame off in slow stretchesThe plate TC label is rounded in slow stretches with fractional frames, so it can be off by one frame.
  • No drop-frame timecodeDrop-frame timecode (29.97 DF) is not supported. Only 23.976, 24, 25 and 30 non-drop are read correctly.
  • Unfamiliar burn-in fonts are untestedBurn-in reading starts from a built-in dictionary of 21 fonts. Unfamiliar fonts (dotted, serif) are untested; if the calibration test covers few frames, type one or two timecodes by hand.
  • MP4 plate TC comes from the fileNuke's movie Reader does not read the timecode track of an MP4 plate (it reports 00:00:00:00 on Sony XAVC), so the plate's first-frame timecode is read from the file's own tmcd or rtmd track. If the file has none, it is estimated from the picture, and on edits dominated by hard cuts that estimate is refused and the timecode is not used.

Cache

Long plates are scanned at a stride first, then only the used range is rendered as small one-channel EXR thumbnails. Advanced settings let you choose and open temporary storage; the system temp folder is the default. ROI masks use persistent local storage by default; a custom temporary location also holds its ROI masks. The tool cleans its own eligible caches. Keep masks referenced by saved scripts and review outputs you want to retain. Source media is never overwritten.

Hard cases

When the picture can’t say which frame

Two situations where matching whole frames falls short: a plate that barely changes, and slow motion the edit made by interpolating. Each has its own warning or note and its own handling. The sets behind the numbers are synthetic test edits with known answers, not production footage.

Hard case 1

Near-still stretches: warn, then join at constant speed

Where the plate barely changes (a held shot, a very slow move), neighboring plate frames look alike and the difference table goes flat. Several paths fit equally well, so the picture cannot say which frame is which. The tool finds these stretches, warns “Frames indistinguishable” and says how many frames the doubt spans (for example ±4), and joins the frames it can trust on either side with a straight, constant-speed line instead of a guess that wobbles.

If a confident burn-in timecode lies inside the stretch, it settles the frames there and no warning is raised. A stretch with only weak evidence gets a gray note, not a warning, unless it has a distinct frame on one side only (or on neither) and the picture matches it poorly: that raises the orange “Insufficient evidence” warning.

Appendix · How it works

Classical image processing that matches the whole shot at once

Every calculation runs inside Nuke on your own machine, in numpy. There is no neural network, no model download and no server. The tool compares brightness order and finds the frame pairs that fit the whole shot best. Six ideas below explain how.

How it works, in detail

Six matching principles, technical thresholds and the Nuke nodes it builds

For the curious. Each part is folded; open the ones you want, or open them all.

Principle 1

Small pictures that ignore the grade

Each frame is reduced to luminance and shrunk (128 px wide for the edit, 256 px for the plate). Every pixel is then replaced by its place in the brightness order, darkest first. A grade that changes brightness without changing the order (gamma, levels, gain, a smooth LUT) leaves those places as they were, so different looks end up as mostly the same picture.

Whatever is not picture is kept out first: black bars, pillar columns, burn-ins, logos and watermarks. Burn-ins and the like are found from the edit alone, as edges that never move. Each comparison also drops the worst 15% of cells, in case something was missed.

Brightness orderThe pixels of a frame are lined up from darkest to brightest and each is replaced by its place in line (scaled to −1…1). Pixels with equal brightness share the average place. Any adjustment that keeps the order (gamma, levels, gain) gives the same result. Technical name: rank normalization Picture areaColumns that stay black through the whole shot (pillarbox) are measured along the middle row. If the remaining picture is at most 70% of the frame wide, matching runs inside that area only. Without this step, a vertical plate fitted small inside the frame would need a scale outside the searched range (0.8–1.6×) and could not be matched. Leave out text and edges that never moveText, logos and bar edges that stay in place while the picture moves are found and left out. Translucent bars at the top and bottom are found by their flat brightness. Found from the edit alone. Technical name: static-edge mask (the high-passed time average is more than 3 times the time deviation) Drop the worst 15% of cellsA frame is split into 8 px cells (at 128 px wide), each cell is compared, and the average is taken after dropping the worst 15%, so a leftover caption or a flash cannot decide the frame. Technical name: trimmed mean
Principle 2

Compare every frame pair, then pick the pairing that fits the whole shot best

Every edit frame is compared with every plate frame. That makes a table, edit frames across and plate frames up, with how different the two pictures are in each cell.

The answer is the path across this table with the smallest total difference. The search begins within 4 plate frames per edit frame and expands to 8 or 16 only when sustained high-speed evidence supports it. A small penalty applies when the speed changes. So speed shows up as the shape of the path: a steady slope is constant speed, a bend is a ramp, a flat stretch is a hold, a downward slope is reverse.

Where the plate barely changes, neighboring plate frames look alike, the table goes flat and several paths fit equally well. Those stretches are found, flagged and joined with a straight line (see Hard cases).

Frame difference tableThe difference between two frames is the average gap between the brightness places of the cells kept. It is 0 for identical pictures and about 0.6 for unrelated ones. For each edit frame the best-matching plate frame is shifted to 0, and the values are divided by the shot’s typical best value, so every edit frame counts the same. Over 300 plate frames, exact values are computed only around the most likely plate frames. Finding the best-fitting path for the whole shotFor every combination of plate frame and last step, only the best-fitting way to get there is kept, and the path is read back from the end. A step is how many plate frames the path advances per edit frame. Because the last step is remembered, only a change of speed can be penalized and everything else follows the picture. Technical name: Viterbi, dynamic programming Adaptive range, up to 16 frames/stepThe first search allows up to 4 plate frames per edit frame, then retries at 8 or 16 only when a sustained fast path is supported. The user ceiling defaults to 16 and can be set from 4 to 16. A negative step is reverse. This is plate frames per edit frame; when the plate and edit have different native frame rates, it is not the same number as real-time playback speed. Snap to a straight line when the speed is constantIf the path stays within 2.5 frames of a straight line, candidate lines with slightly different speed (±0.02) and start position are rounded to whole frames and scored on picture difference alone. The best line replaces the path only when it fits better. This keeps 0.5× and 1.5× edits from wobbling between runs of 1,1,1 and 0,0.
Principle 3

Reframes: one transform, or a moving one

Before any frame is matched, the tool looks for one fixed Transform that lays the plate onto the edit. It searches scale 0.8× to 1.6×, a shift of up to 20% of the width, and optionally a mirror (flop). The search starts coarse, narrows step by step, and is checked on several frame pairs. Rotation is not searched.

A zoom that changes during the shot is handled second, once a path exists. The tool finds a key every 4 frames and joins the keys into curves. It accepts a zoom track only when separate validation supports it, then matches frames again with the tracked transform. Reframe output (Beta), off by default, can export accepted scale/position keys to a separate Transform.

The curve must fit better than the fixed scale by an average correlation of 0.03 on at least 80% of those frames (or 0.08 on 60%), or pass a second test that compares only the frames where the curve and the fixed scale differ by more than 1%. Principle 4

The path never jumps at a cut; it only flags it

A retime is a speed curve. A jump inside the shot is an editing decision, not a speed, so the path is not allowed to jump. A jump with no warning would put a wrong TimeWarp in the script with nobody noticing. This is by design, not because the tool cannot find the jump.

To find suspect spots, the same difference table is calculated a second time with jumps allowed. A warning goes on the first frame after the spot when the speed-only path fits clearly worse than the jump path, when six or more frames in a row match nothing in the plate, or when a confident burn-in timecode jumps. The path stays continuous, so that stretch needs a look: pin a frame or exclude the range, then run again.

Cut suspicion scorePer frame: the difference on the speed-only path minus the difference on the jump path, plus the penalty for changing speed. The unit is set so that an unrelated picture is 1. A cluster of frames whose sum passes 2.0 is reported as a suspected cut. Technical name: excess cost Another sourceSix or more frames in a row that match nothing in this plate suggest a cut to another source. The report says the picture after the cut is not in this plate. Known gapA jump of only two or three frames is not flagged, and on real footage 4 and 8 frame jumps went unflagged without a readable timecode.
Principle 5

Read the burn-in timecode, then check it against the picture

You mark the burn-in’s timecode area once. Each digit of HH:MM:SS:FF is cut from a fixed grid and compared with stored digit shapes: a built-in set for 21 fonts, plus shapes the clip taught the reader itself. The tool then picks the likeliest timecode for every frame across the clip. It does not assume +1 per frame, so holds, ramps, reverse and jumps can be read too; any change carries a penalty, so it must be well supported. A frame’s value is reported only when it beats the alternatives by a clear margin; otherwise that frame gets no value.

The plate’s own timecode is read straight from the file’s QuickTime or Sony XAVC timecode track, because Nuke’s movie reader often reports 00:00:00:00 for MP4. When confident readings cover enough of the edit and fall inside the plate, they become fixed points on the path, and less sure ones act as weak hints. If the picture and a reading disagree, the picture wins: a reading it clearly contradicts is dropped, and if the disagreement turns out to be an offset or a mismatch, timecode is not used for that shot and a warning says so.

Principle 6

A match score, separate warnings, and the nodes it builds

The match score turns the median frame difference into a percentage: 100% is identical pictures, 0% is what two unrelated pictures score. A good average can hide a bad stretch, so warnings are shown apart from the score. They cover four cases: frames with an equally good match far away in the plate, frames whose difference is over 2.5 times the median, an upper or lower half of the picture that disagrees with the whole, and near-still plates where a time shift cannot be told from camera drift.

The path then becomes nodes: a TimeWarp keyed by plate frame number (decimal numbers in interpolated slow stretches), FrameRange, Reformat, a Transform for scale, shift and flop, and a QC branch that shows the difference between the edit and the retimed plate. When the look match validates, an enabled Grade NMMR_look_* follows the Transform. Everything sits in one backdrop and undoes with one Ctrl+Z; each plate gets one StickyNote, and its full report is in the NMMR Report tab of the TimeWarp.

Match scorematch score = 100 × (1 − frame difference / 0.60), limited to 0–100, where the frame difference is the median over the shot’s frames (0 for identical pictures, about 0.6 for unrelated ones). Good: difference up to 0.09 (about 85%). Fair: up to 0.18 (about 70%). Above that the shot is marked “Review required”. Confidence warningsShown when any of these holds: over 20% of frames are ambiguous (another candidate far from the path scores within 3% of the best), over 30% of frames have a difference above 2.5 times the median, or the upper and lower halves, matched separately, agree with the path on under 70% of frames. Near-still platesWhen a plate frame and the one four later fit each other by a position shift alone (correlation of 0.97 or more), the plate carries little time information. There, a zoom or drift cannot be told from a time difference, so the tool warns “time/position ambiguous” (cannot tell a time difference from camera movement) and the path may wobble.
